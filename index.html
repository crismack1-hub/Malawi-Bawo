How to play

The board

32 pits in 4 rows of 8. Each player owns one half (16 pits) — an inner row and an outer row. Every pit starts with 2 seeds.

Sowing

Pick one of your pits that has 2 or more seeds. Pick a direction — clockwise or anti-clockwise. Then lift all the seeds and drop one in each pit going around your own two rows.

Marker pits

A marker pit is a pit on your inner row whose opposite (the opponent's inner-row pit at the same column) contains seeds. Marker pits are highlighted.

Capturing — mtaji turn

If your last seed lands in a marker pit, capture all of the opponent's seeds in the opposite pit. Then sow those captured seeds in the same direction, starting from where you captured. Each new sowing can capture again — the chain continues until a sowing doesn't capture.

Kimbi rule

The first two and last two pits of your inner row are kimbis. The corner pit at each end is a kichwa. If you capture in a kimbi, the captured seeds are sown from the kichwa instead of from the kimbi:

  • Capture in a right kimbi (column 6 or 7) → sow anti-clockwise from the right kichwa (column 7).
  • Capture in a left kimbi (column 0 or 1) → sow clockwise from the left kichwa (column 0).

The opponent's kimbis are highlighted on the board.

Takata turn

If your first sowing doesn't capture, no capture can occur in the rest of the turn — the move ends. This is called a takata turn.

Mandatory capture

If any of your possible moves captures, you must play a capturing move. If several captures are available, you pick which one.

End of the game

You lose on your turn if your inner row is empty or if no pit on your side has 2 or more seeds.
